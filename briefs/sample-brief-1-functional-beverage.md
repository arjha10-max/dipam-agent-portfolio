## 2. Business context

Vividance is preparing to launch **Hydration + Focus**, a ready-to-drink functional hydration beverage for adults who want a simple daily hydration ritual without a heavy energy-drink positioning.

The fictional product concept combines electrolytes, magnesium, and L-theanine. The brand wants to avoid medicinal language, disease claims, and overstated cognitive claims.

The launch challenge is to establish a distinctive position between plain water enhancement, sports hydration, and energy drinks. The campaign must recruit new buyers while giving current category buyers a reason to switch.

## 3. Objectives

### Business objective

Build a sustainable launch base by driving trial and repeat purchase in Canada and the United States during the first 12 months.

### Marketing objective

Position Vividance as the calm, everyday hydration choice for people with active routines and mentally demanding days.

### Media objective

Create efficient reach among priority audiences, then concentrate spend on audiences, retailers, creative variants, and occasions showing the strongest qualified response.

### Measurement objective

Establish a learning agenda that connects audience exposure, creative response, retailer behavior, and repeat purchase without relying on raw PII movement.

## 8. Audience hypotheses

### Current buyers

Adults aged 25–44 who already buy functional beverages, sports drinks, enhanced water, or better-for-you ready-to-drink beverages.

### Growth audiences

1. Active professionals who want a hydration ritual during workdays, commuting, travel, or exercise-adjacent occasions.
2. Wellness-curious shoppers who are reducing energy-drink use but still want a functional beverage cue.

### Audiences to exclude

- Children and teens
- Audiences built from sensitive health inferences
- Users who have withdrawn consent or are not eligible for activation in a given market

### Audience data available

Synthetic first-party email list, website analytics, retailer clean-room audience estimates, and contextual category signals.
